Payout destinations

A destination is a wallet you register once and then pay to repeatedly. Because registering one decides where your money goes, each new destination sits in a security cooldown before its first payout — that window is your chance to notice and undo an unwanted change. Your other payout methods keep working normally throughout.

Goblin (Nostr)

Enter the Goblin username your wallet answers to. Re-registering replaces the current destination and restarts the cooldown — so if someone who guessed your ownership proof registered their own name, registering yours evicts theirs before it can ever go active.

This one action needs both proofs. Tor and Slatepack can only ever pay your own mining address, so proving ownership there moves nothing. A Goblin username is a different address — so changing it asks for your mining IP and your rig password, not either one.

P-08 Withdrawal history every payout, kept forever ·

Your payout requests only — no donations or clawbacks. Unlike the ledgers above (row detail kept 60 days), this list goes back to your first payout. Export the full history as CSV for your records.

Proofs are hidden by default. A payout's kernel identifies its transaction in the Grin chain forever, so publishing it beside an address would let anyone link your payouts to the chain. Enter your ownership proof at the top of this page and press Reveal proofs: every mined payout gets an explorer link (proof ↗, the kernel — proves the transaction was mined), and every Tor payout also gets a ⬇ file — the signed payment proof. That file carries your own wallet's signature over the amount, the kernel and the sender, so it proves to anyone, with no trust in this pool, that your wallet received exactly this payment. Compare the kernel with grin-wallet txs on your side, or check the file with grin-wallet verify_proof. Slatepack payouts carry no signed proof — you finalised those yourself, and their kernel is the evidence.
