Site notices and maintenance banners

Maintenance Mode

Shows a branded full-page notice to visitors. It is a website notice only — miners keep mining. To stop miner intake use Stratum below: a stratum pause also shows this overlay, with the text set at the bottom of this card, whenever this switch is off. It never changes this switch. The admin panel stays accessible, and the login and account pages stay reachable, so miners can still check their balance.
Used instead of the title and message above while stratum is paused and the switch above is off. The overlay adds “Expected back at about … UTC” itself. Leave both blank for the defaults. Saved with Save Changes; text only — it cannot pause or resume anything.

Stratum

Stops miner intake: the public stratum port and every regional listener close, shares already in flight are recorded first (up to 30 s), then every miner is disconnected and refused until you resume. Miners fail over to their backup pool and come back on their own. Use it around a restore, a hub move or database trouble. Every pause resumes on its own after the time you pick (2 h at most; extend if you need longer). Payouts are not touched — they have their own freeze under Payouts. The buttons here act at once and are not part of Save Changes. All times UTC.

Planned window

A one-off pause that starts and ends on its own — for maintenance you can plan. It must start at least 15 min from now and within 30 days, and last 5 min to 6 h. Miners see a banner from 24 h before it starts. One window at a time: scheduling another replaces it. Once it has started it is an ordinary pause — end it early with Resume now.
Moving the hub (Migrate OUT / IN) or restoring a backup — the order that keeps downtime short

The pool menu's B → 6 Migrate OUT and B → 7 Migrate IN stop and start the service themselves. Pausing first adds the one thing a plain stop cannot: every share and block candidate in flight is recorded before the old hub goes dark. Downtime is set by the steps between the pause and the resume on the new hub, so keep that stretch short.

  1. Days before, on the new box: pool menu B → 7 → CHECK until it says READY. Fix everything there — outside the downtime.
  2. Old hub, here: Pause stratum for 60 min, reason “hub move”. Wait for “settled — safe to proceed”.
  3. Old hub: B → 6 Migrate OUT straight away. Payouts freeze as part of it.
  4. New box: B → 7 → MIGRATE. The pool comes up paused for up to 2 hours from its start, however long the move took — Migrate IN writes the pause into the restored database right before it starts the service. If the strip is not red once it is up, pause it here before you do anything else.
  5. Change exactly the DNS records Migrate IN lists.
  6. New hub, here: once its continuity checks are all ✓, press Resume now — do not wait for the automatic resume, that wait is pure downtime. Check that every listener reads listening. A region that fails with EADDRNOTAVAIL means its WireGuard address is not up yet: bring the tunnel up, then press Re-bind.
  7. Watch every gateway reach ✓ on the Regions page. Payouts stay frozen until you have reconciled — then resume them under Payouts.

Never resume the old hub after Migrate OUT — two live pools on one wallet seed. A rollback (valid only until the new hub has accepted a share) brings the old hub back still paused until its resume time; resume it here once it is up. A plain restore (B menu restore) pauses it too, for 2 hours from the restore — the menu prints the resume time. Start the pool within that, check it, then follow step 6; a pool started after it comes up accepting, so pause it here first.

Announcement Banners

This is the only place banners come from. Everything shown at the top of a public page is a banner in this list — including the “testing phase” notice a new pool starts with. Edit or disable it here when you go live; there is no second banner setting anywhere else in Settings. Once this section has been saved, the saved list is what shows — a newer shipped default no longer applies.
Short messages shown site-wide at the top of every public page. Each banner has a type, optional link, on/off toggle, optional dismiss button, and an optional start/end date.
